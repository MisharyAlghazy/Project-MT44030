\section{T-stiffener versus L-stiffener}
By shifting the flange to an off-center position the center of rotation also moves, due to the non-symmetry of the new section. which results in more warping during torsional loading, which means that the torsional resistance will be less.

This can also shown by calculating the maximum torsional load for each individual part of the equation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{t,\mathrm{open\ Web}}
&= \frac{\tau_{\mathrm{open},1}\left(600b^2t_p+175t_p^2\right)}{21} \rightarrow 28.57 \tau_{\mathrm{open}}b^2

&= \frac{\tau_{\mathrm{closed},1}\left(120b^2t_p+35t_p^3\right)}{6} \rightarrow 20b^2t_p\tau_{closed}b^2
\end{aligned}
\]
From this can be concluded that maximum torsional moment is largest for the webs and the smallest for the flanges, with the outer plating in between.
