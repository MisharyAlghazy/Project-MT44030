\chapter{Closed Cells in Sections B}
\label{ch:assignment3}

\begingroup\itshape Assignment 3 (19 points)\endgroup

\bigskip
The three configurations are a box of $5b\times2b$ with stiffeners on the top and bottom plates. The dimensions are read from Figures~7--9 of the assignment:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Large T-stiffeners (configuration 1, and the five top stiffeners of configuration 2): web $b/2\times t_p$, flange $b/4\times2t_p$.
  \item Trapezoids (configurations 2 and 3): base $5b/6$, crown $b/3$ with thickness $t_p$, legs at $45^\circ$ with thickness $t_p/2$ and length $\tfrac{\sqrt2}{4}b$, height $b/4$. The enclosed area is $\Omega_t = \tfrac12\left(\tfrac{5b}{6}+\tfrac{b}{3}\right)\tfrac{b}{4} = \tfrac{7}{48}b^2$.
  \item Small T-stiffener on the crown (configuration 2): web $b/4\times t_p$, flange $b/4\times t_p$.
\end{itemize}
The thickness of the box plating is not given in the figures. We found it by recalculating configuration 3 with the method below. With plating $2t_p$ (and $t_p$ for the vertical webs) we get $I_{p,3}=61.3\,b^3t_p$ and $M_{t,3}=36.3\,b^2t_p\tau_{max}$, within 0.5\,\% of the given values. With plating $t_p$ we would get $I_{p,3}=32.4\,b^3t_p$, which is a factor two off. So in this chapter the box plating is $2t_p$.

\section{Shear flow sketch for configurations 1 and 2}
\label{sec:a3-1}

\paragraph{Configuration 1.}
The box is one closed cell, so the shear flow $q$ is constant along all four plates. The T-stiffeners are open sections. They have a linear stress over their thickness, but no net shear flow.

\paragraph{Configuration 2.}
The trapezoids form three extra closed cells together with the bottom plate. The main cell has flow $q_m$ and each trapezoid has flow $q_t$, both counter-clockwise. In the legs and crown of the trapezoids the two flows go against each other, so only $q_m - q_t$ is left there. The bottom plate under a trapezoid only carries $q_t$.

\section{T-stiffener versus L-stiffener}
\label{sec:a3-2}

For open sections the torsion stiffness is the sum over the separate plates, $I_{p,o} = \frac13\sum_j b_{p,j}\,t_{p,j}^3$. As shown in the lectures, the orientation and location of the plates in the cross-section do not matter for this. An L-stiffener with the same web and flange has the same plates as the T-stiffener, so its contribution stays the same: $\frac13\left(\frac{b}{2}t_p^3 + \frac{b}{4}(2t_p)^3\right) = \frac56\,bt_p^3$ per stiffener.

\finalanswer{There is no change: the open-section stiffness $\tfrac13\sum b_pt_p^3$ only depends on the plate dimensions, not on where the flange is.}

\section{Shear flow components}
\label{sec:a3-3}

Both configurations are hybrid sections: closed cells plus open stiffeners. From the lectures, with the same twist rate $\theta$ for all parts:
\begin{equation}
  \{\mathbf{q}_c\} = \frac{M_t}{2}\cdot\frac{[\mathbf{S}]^{-1}\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\}}{\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\}^T[\mathbf{S}]^{-1}\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\} + \frac{1}{12}\{\mathbf{A}_p\}^T\{\mathbf{t}_p^2\}},
  \qquad
  \{\mathbf{q}_o\} = \frac{M_t}{4}\cdot\frac{\{\mathbf{t}_p^2\}}{\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\}^T[\mathbf{S}]^{-1}\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\} + \frac{1}{12}\{\mathbf{A}_p\}^T\{\mathbf{t}_p^2\}}
  \label{eq:a3-q}
\end{equation}
where $\{\mathbf{A}_p\}$ contains $b_{p,j}t_{p,j}$ of every open plate (web and flange separately) and $\{\mathbf{t}_p^2\}$ their thickness squared.

\paragraph{Configuration 1.}
One cell with $\Omega = 5b\cdot2b = 10b^2$ and $S = \oint\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{t_p} = \frac{14b}{2t_p} = 7\frac{b}{t_p}$, so $\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\}^T[\mathbf{S}]^{-1}\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\} = \frac{100}{7}b^3t_p$.
The open part is 10 webs and 10 flanges:
\begin{equation*}
  \{\mathbf{A}_p\}^T = \frac{bt_p}{2}\,[\,1\ \cdots\ 1\,]_{1\times20}, \qquad
  \{\mathbf{t}_p^2\}^T = t_p^2\,[\,\underbrace{1\ \cdots\ 1}_{\text{10 webs}}\ \underbrace{4\ \cdots\ 4}_{\text{10 flanges}}\,]
  \quad\rightarrow\quad
  \tfrac{1}{12}\{\mathbf{A}_p\}^T\{\mathbf{t}_p^2\} = \tfrac{25}{12}\,bt_p^3 .
\end{equation*}
With Eq.~\eqref{eq:a3-q}:
\begin{equation}
  q_c = \frac{12\,M_t}{240\,b^2 + 35\,t_p^2} \approx 0.0500\,\frac{M_t}{b^2},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  q_{o,web} = \frac{21\,M_t\,t_p}{1200\,b^3 + 175\,b\,t_p^2} \approx 0.0175\,\frac{M_tt_p}{b^3},
  \qquad
  q_{o,flange} = \frac{84\,M_t\,t_p}{1200\,b^3 + 175\,b\,t_p^2} \approx 0.0700\,\frac{M_tt_p}{b^3}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Configuration 2.}
Four cells: the main cell (1) and the three trapezoids (2--4). The areas are
\begin{equation*}
  \{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\} = b^2\left\{10-3\cdot\tfrac{7}{48},\ \tfrac{7}{48},\ \tfrac{7}{48},\ \tfrac{7}{48}\right\}^T
  = b^2\left\{9.5625,\ 0.1458,\ 0.1458,\ 0.1458\right\}^T .
\end{equation*}
For the contour integrals: the main cell has $11.5b$ of plating at $2t_p$ and runs over the legs and crown of the three trapezoids; a trapezoid cell has its base ($5b/6$, $2t_p$), two legs and the crown; the legs and crown are shared:
\begin{align*}
  S_{11} &= \frac{11.5}{2} + 3\left(2\cdot\frac{\sqrt2/4}{1/2} + \frac{1/3}{1}\right) = \frac{27}{4} + 3\sqrt2 = 10.99\,\frac{b}{t_p},\\
  S_{ii} &= \frac{5/6}{2} + \sqrt2 + \frac13 = \frac34 + \sqrt2 = 2.164\,\frac{b}{t_p}, \qquad
  S_{1i} = -\left(\sqrt2 + \frac13\right) = -1.748\,\frac{b}{t_p} \qquad (i=2,3,4),
\end{align*}
\begin{equation*}
  [\mathbf{S}] = \frac{b}{t_p}
  \begin{bmatrix}
    10.99 & -1.748 & -1.748 & -1.748 \\
    -1.748 & 2.164 & 0 & 0 \\
    -1.748 & 0 & 2.164 & 0 \\
    -1.748 & 0 & 0 & 2.164
  \end{bmatrix}
  \ \rightarrow\
  [\mathbf{S}]^{-1}\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\} = bt_p\left\{1.4670,\ 1.2519,\ 1.2519,\ 1.2519\right\}^T,
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
  \{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\}^T[\mathbf{S}]^{-1}\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\} = 14.576\,b^3t_p .
\end{equation*}
The open part is 5 large T's on the deck and 3 small T's on the crowns:
\begin{gather*}
  \{\mathbf{A}_p\}^T = bt_p\,[\,\underbrace{\tfrac12\ \cdots\ \tfrac12}_{\text{5 webs, 5 flanges}}\ \underbrace{\tfrac14\ \cdots\ \tfrac14}_{\text{3 webs, 3 flanges}}\,],
  \qquad
  \{\mathbf{t}_p^2\}^T = t_p^2\,[\,\underbrace{1\ \cdots\ 1}_{\text{5}}\ \underbrace{4\ \cdots\ 4}_{\text{5}}\ \underbrace{1\ \cdots\ 1}_{\text{6}}\,]\\
  \rightarrow\quad
  \tfrac{1}{12}\{\mathbf{A}_p\}^T\{\mathbf{t}_p^2\} = \tfrac{7}{6}\,bt_p^3 .
\end{gather*}
With Eq.~\eqref{eq:a3-q}:
\begin{equation}
  \{\mathbf{q}_c\} = \frac{M_t\,bt_p}{2\left(14.576\,b^3t_p + 1.167\,bt_p^3\right)}
  \begin{Bmatrix} 1.4670\\ 1.2519\\ 1.2519\\ 1.2519 \end{Bmatrix}
  \approx \frac{M_t}{b^2}\begin{Bmatrix} 0.0503\\ 0.0429\\ 0.0429\\ 0.0429 \end{Bmatrix},
\end{equation}
\begin{gather}
  q_{o,j} = \frac{M_t\,t_{p,j}^2}{4\left(14.576\,b^3t_p + 1.167\,bt_p^3\right)}:\\
  q_{o,web} \approx 0.0172\,\frac{M_tt_p}{b^3}\ \text{(all webs and small flanges)},
  \qquad
  q_{o,flange} \approx 0.0686\,\frac{M_tt_p}{b^3}\ \text{(large flanges)} .\nonumber
\end{gather}

\finalanswer{Configuration 1: $q_c = \dfrac{12M_t}{240b^2+35t_p^2}\approx0.0500\,\dfrac{M_t}{b^2}$, $q_{o,web}\approx0.0175\,\dfrac{M_tt_p}{b^3}$, $q_{o,flange}\approx0.0700\,\dfrac{M_tt_p}{b^3}$.\\
Configuration 2: $q_m = q_1\approx0.0503\,\dfrac{M_t}{b^2}$, $q_t = q_{2,3,4}\approx0.0429\,\dfrac{M_t}{b^2}$, so $q_m-q_t\approx0.0074\,\dfrac{M_t}{b^2}$ in the legs and crowns; $q_{o,web}\approx0.0172\,\dfrac{M_tt_p}{b^3}$, $q_{o,flange}\approx0.0686\,\dfrac{M_tt_p}{b^3}$.}

\section{Maximum shear stress}
\label{sec:a3-4}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*., leftmargin=*]
  \item In every wall $\tau = q/t_p$, with the net flow in walls between two cells. In the open plates the stress is linear over the thickness and $\tau = q_o/t_p$ is the value at the surface.

  \textbf{Configuration 1.} In the box plating ($2t_p$):
  \begin{equation*}
    \tau_c = \frac{q_c}{2t_p} = \frac{6\,M_t}{240\,b^2t_p + 35\,t_p^3} \approx 0.0250\,\frac{M_t}{b^2t_p} .
  \end{equation*}
  In the stiffeners: $\tau_{o,web} = q_{o,web}/t_p \approx 0.0175\,M_t/b^3$ and $\tau_{o,flange} = q_{o,flange}/2t_p \approx 0.0350\,M_t/b^3$. This is a factor $1.4\,t_p/b$ of the stress in the plating, so for $b\gg t_p$ the stiffeners are not critical.

  \textbf{Configuration 2.} The walls of the closed part:
  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    Wall & $t$ & $q_{net}$ [$M_t/b^2$] & $\tau$ [$M_t/(b^2t_p)$] \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{Deck, sides, bottom between trapezoids} & $\boldsymbol{2t_p}$ & $\boldsymbol{q_m = 0.0503}$ & $\boldsymbol{0.0252}$ \\
    Bottom under a trapezoid                & $2t_p$   & $q_t = 0.0429$     & $0.0215$ \\
    Trapezoid legs                          & $t_p/2$  & $q_m-q_t = 0.0074$ & $0.0148$ \\
    Trapezoid crown                         & $t_p$    & $q_m-q_t = 0.0074$ & $0.0074$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  The stiffeners again give only $\mathcal{O}(t_p/b)$ of this.

  \finalanswer{Configuration 1: $\tau_{max,1} \approx 0.0250\,\dfrac{M_t}{b^2t_p}$, constant in the whole box plating (deck, sides and bottom).\\
  Configuration 2: $\tau_{max,2} \approx 0.0252\,\dfrac{M_t}{b^2t_p}$, in the box plating of the main cell (deck, sides and the bottom between the trapezoids).}

  \item Compared with the given value of configuration 3:
  \begin{equation*}
    \tau_{max,1} = 0.0250\,\frac{M_t}{b^2t_p},\qquad
    \tau_{max,2} = 0.0252\,\frac{M_t}{b^2t_p},\qquad
    \tau_{max,3} = 0.0277\,\frac{M_t}{b^2t_p}.
  \end{equation*}
  Configurations 1 and 2 are almost the same, because in both the torque is carried by one large flow around the box, $q\approx M_t/(2\cdot10b^2)$. Configuration 3 has about 10\,\% higher stress, even though it has more closed cells. The vertical webs split the box into four cells, and the two middle cells get a higher flow than the outer cells. The deck and bottom plating of these middle cells carry this higher flow. So adding cells does not always lower the maximum stress.
\end{enumerate}

\section{(Specific) torsion stiffness and strength}
\label{sec:a3-5}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*., leftmargin=*]
  \item From the lectures, $I_p = 4\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\}^T[\mathbf{S}]^{-1}\{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\} + \frac13\{\mathbf{A}_p\}^T\{\mathbf{t}_p^2\}$. The strength follows from setting the maximum stress of Question~\ref{sec:a3-4} equal to $\tau_{max}$.

  \textbf{Configuration 1.}
  \begin{gather*}
    I_{p,1} = \frac{400}{7}\,b^3t_p + \frac{25}{3}\,bt_p^3 = 57.14\,b^3t_p + 8.33\,bt_p^3,\\
    M_{t,1} = \frac{240\,b^2t_p + 35\,t_p^3}{6}\,\tau_{max} = \left(40.0\,b^2t_p + 5.83\,t_p^3\right)\tau_{max}.
  \end{gather*}

  \textbf{Configuration 2.}
  \begin{gather*}
    I_{p,2} = 4\cdot14.576\,b^3t_p + \frac{14}{3}\,bt_p^3 = 58.30\,b^3t_p + 4.67\,bt_p^3,\\
    M_{t,2} = \frac{4\left(14.576\,b^2t_p + 1.167\,t_p^3\right)}{1.4670}\,\tau_{max} = \left(39.7\,b^2t_p + 3.18\,t_p^3\right)\tau_{max}.
  \end{gather*}

  For the \emph{specific} values we divide by the material area of the cross-section, $A$ (box plating $28\,bt_p$ plus the stiffeners; for configuration 3 the webs are taken as $t_p$ thick). For $b\gg t_p$:
  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
     & config.\ 1 & config.\ 2 & config.\ 3 (given) \\
    \midrule
    $I_p$ [$b^3t_p$]                    & 57.1  & 58.3  & 61.6 \\
    $M_t$ [$b^2t_p\tau_{max}$]          & 40.0  & 39.7  & 36.1 \\
    $A$ [$bt_p$]                        & 38.0  & 36.6  & 36.6 \\
    $I_p/A$ [$b^2$]                     & 1.50  & 1.59  & 1.68 \\
    $M_t/A$ [$b\,\tau_{max}$]           & 1.05  & 1.09  & 0.99 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}

  \finalanswer{$I_{p,1} = 57.1\,b^3t_p + 8.33\,bt_p^3$, $M_{t,1} = 40.0\,b^2t_p\tau_{max}$; $I_{p,2} = 58.3\,b^3t_p + 4.67\,bt_p^3$, $M_{t,2} = 39.7\,b^2t_p\tau_{max}$ (for $b\gg t_p$). Specific values: see the table above.}

  \item \emph{Stiffness.} Configuration 3 is 8\,\% stiffer than configuration 1 and 6\,\% stiffer than configuration 2. Per unit of material the gap with configuration 1 is even larger (12\,\%), because configuration 1 uses the most material. All three have the same box, which gives $57.1\,b^3t_p$. Adding closed walls inside the box increases $I_p$: three small cells at the bottom (configuration 2) give 2\,\%, while the webs of configuration 3 divide the whole box and give more.

  \emph{Strength.} Configuration 3 is about 10\,\% weaker than 1 and 2, because the flow in the middle cells is higher than in the outer cells (Question~\ref{sec:a3-4}b). Per unit of material, configuration 2 is the strongest, because it uses less material than configuration 1 for the same strength.

  \item The twist rate is the same for the open and closed parts, so the torque is divided in the same ratio as the stiffness:
  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
     & config.\ 1 & config.\ 2 \\
    \midrule
    $I_{p,closed}$ & $57.14\,b^3t_p$ & $58.30\,b^3t_p$ \\
    $I_{p,open}$   & $8.33\,bt_p^3$  & $4.67\,bt_p^3$ \\
    $M_{t,open}/M_{t,closed} = I_{p,open}/I_{p,closed}$ & $0.146\,(t_p/b)^2$ & $0.080\,(t_p/b)^2$ \\
    example $b/t_p = 50$ & $0.006\,\%$ & $0.003\,\%$ \\
    $\tau_{open,max}/\tau_{closed,max}$ & $1.40\,t_p/b$ & $1.36\,t_p/b$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  The open stiffeners give only a part of order $(t_p/b)^2$ of the stiffness and torque, and their maximum stress is only $\approx1.4\,t_p/b$ of the stress in the closed walls. So the closed cells set both the stiffness and the strength. In configuration 2 the open part is even smaller, because three bottom T-stiffeners are replaced by trapezoids with smaller T's.
\end{enumerate}
